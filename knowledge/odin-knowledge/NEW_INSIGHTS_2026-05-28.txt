## [SMS "DOUBLE WHAMMY SCRIPT" — Carrier Bypass System]
**Source:** 2026-05-28_SMS Text Blasting.txt
**Insight:** SMS carriers block texts containing real estate keywords (cash, house,
property, sell). The bypass is a two-text system:
  Text 1 (ONLY goal: get a reply):
    "Hey, this is [name]. I just drove by your place at [address] and
    something seemed off. Is everything alright?"
  Why it works: Address makes every text unique — carriers cannot flag it
  as duplicate spam. A reply from the recipient overrides the spam filter
  permanently for that thread.
  Text 2 (send AFTER they reply ANYTHING):
    "Hey, I was just seeing if you're interested in selling your house for
    cash. I'm looking to buy a couple more properties in the area."
  BONUS: Using a woman's name in Text 1 increases reply rate by 20%.
  GOOGLE VOICE FREE RULE: Max 5 texts per hour per account. Use 6 accounts
  simultaneously = 30 texts/hour = 300 texts/day for free.
  PAID SMS RATE: $0.0087/text (~$8.70 per 1,000 texts) via XLeads Ninja Mode.
  Opt-out language hack: "Stop owning your house" counts as legal opt-out language
  with XLeads Ninja Mode.

## [GOVERNMENT LIST ACQUISITION — Exact Tactics for Resistant Offices]
**Source:** 2026-05-28_Government Lists.txt
**Insight:** Most government offices initially refuse to give lists. Exact tactics:

  TACTIC 1 — Play dumb, walk them to the answer:
    Do not say: "Give me the probate list."
    Say: "What happens when someone dies and has a house? Does the government take it?"
    They explain. You: "Oh, what is that court filing called?"
    They say "probate." You: "Great, how do I access those records?"

  TACTIC 2 — Request court dockets instead of the list:
    Court dockets are schedules of upcoming court cases — legally must be posted.
    All probates, pre-foreclosures (lis pendens), and liens appear on dockets.
    Works when they claim no organized list exists.

  TACTIC 3 — FOIA as leverage (not as a filing):
    "I would hate to have to file a Freedom of Information Act request and
    involve attorneys if you deny it — that would mean you are withholding
    public government information, which is a federal law violation."
    Works best on water/utility departments. They do not want legal liability.

  TACTIC 4 — The newspaper threat for water shutoffs:
    "So you're telling me nobody in this city has to pay their water bill
    because you don't even keep records of who pays?"
    When they backpedal: "Oh, so there IS a list. How do I access it?"

  TACTIC 5 — College student or school project framing:
    If young-looking: "I am doing a school/college project."
    If older: "My son/daughter is doing a research project and I'm helping."
    Government employees consistently respond well to helping students.

  RESISTANCE LEVELS (1=easy, 10=hard to obtain):
    Pre-foreclosures: 2 | Divorce: 3 | Liens: 3 | Arrest records: 4
    Fire damage: 4 | Code violations: 5-7 | Probates: 6
    Tax delinquencies: 6 | Evictions: 6 | Water shutoffs: 8

  USE CHATGPT to process raw list data:
    Screenshot the court docket → upload to ChatGPT →
    "Organize this into a spreadsheet, only include entries with real property associated."
    Replaces $5/hour VA work for data entry. Takes seconds.

  HOW PROBATES WORK IN PRACTICE (step by step):
    1. Go to county clerk / official records website
    2. Search document type: "Probate"
    3. Filter to entries that have a property/legal description
    4. Note the deceased person's name and property address
    5. Go to county property appraiser → search the deceased person's name
       → confirm the address
    6. Go to CyberBackgroundChecks.com → enter the property address
       → find living relatives (son/daughter/heir)
    7. Call the heir (not the deceased)
    XLeads can do steps 5-7 in bulk instantly.

  PRE-FORECLOSURE WINDOW:
    Month 1-3: Missed payments (no public record yet — cannot access)
    Month 4 (~120 days): Bank files lis pendens → NOW it is public record
    Window between lis pendens filing and auction date = YOUR wholesaling window
    Best to approach BEFORE auction date is set — after that sellers procrastinate
    until 3-7 days before auction. Call/text early in the window.
    Do NOT call pre-foreclosures — they are over-called by banks and servicers.
    TEXT them instead.

  SHELBY COUNTY (Memphis) NOTE: Tennessee has rural counties where probates
  are not online. Request the court docket in person or by phone.
  Pre-foreclosures (lis pendens) are public record via Shelby County Register
  of Deeds. Tax delinquencies via Shelby County Trustee.

## [SELLER CONVERSATION — Non-Verbal Communication Framework]
**Source:** 2026-05-28_Seller Conversations.txt
**Insight:** Rick Ginn's non-verbal framework for seller calls and appointments
(applies to virtual/phone just as much as in-person):

  1. TONALITY: Vary pitch and energy. Monotone = no trust. Smile physically
     while on the phone — people can hear it. Match enthusiasm to the context.
  2. SPEED: Mirror the seller's pace exactly.
     Fast talker → speed up. Slow Southern drawl (Memphis) → slow down.
  3. POSTURE: Stand during calls — confidence transfers directly to voice.
     "Your backbone is connected to your tonality."
  4. WORD TIMING (most important in negotiations):
     After making a verbal offer → go SILENT. Could be 2 full minutes of silence.
     Do NOT fill the silence — they are processing. Interrupting kills the deal.
     When they counter, match their response time. If they took 30 seconds to
     counter, take at least 30 seconds to respond — even if you'd accept instantly.
     "If you respond instantly, they think they left money on the table and cancel."
     Make them feel they negotiated hard. They need to believe they got the best deal.

  65% of decision-making is non-verbal (timing, tone, posture) — not words.

  FOLLOW-UP TIMING — refined from original notes:
    30 days or less: daily priority
    30-60 days: weekly contact
    60-90 days: bi-weekly contact
    90-180 days: every 30 days
    180+ days: every 30-45 days

  DECEMBER INSIGHT: End-of-year follow-ups convert at unusually high rates.
  People make decisions in December (new year resolutions, wanting closure).
  All follow-ups set up throughout the year compound into December deals.
  Rick has seen multiple years where December alone closes 20-30% of annual deals.

## [CONDITIONING STATEMENT — Exact Word-for-Word Script]
**Source:** 2026-05-28_Conditioning Sellers.txt
**Insight:** Rick Ginn's exact conditioning/framing script. Use BEFORE every
appointment — both over the phone AND again in person at the start of the walkthrough:

  SCRIPT:
    "Mr./Ms. Seller, before I come see the house, I want to be respectful of your time.
    I am going to come in, take a look at the property, and walk around with you.
    When we are done, you will have a decision to make. Either you are going to say,
    'I really like what you are offering, I want to move forward' — that is a definitive yes.
    Or you are going to say, 'I do not like your offer, I do not want to do this' —
    that is a definitive no. I would like us to make that decision together on the day
    of the appointment. Can I get your commitment to give me a yes or no so we can
    either move forward or walk away as friends? Either way, I am going to help you."
  Zip your lip after the last sentence. Wait for their commitment.

  WHEN THEY GIVE "MAYBE" ANYWAY, use the call-back:
    "Remember before we met, we agreed to give a yes or no today.
    What information are you missing? What part of this is not clear?
    What can I help you with?"

  WHY "MAYBE" IS THE WORST OUTCOME:
    Maybe = either no motivation OR no trust (usually no trust).
    A no is better — you can redirect and find the real objection.
    People who give "maybe" without challenge will shop your offer to other buyers.
    Without the conditioning statement, you have no leverage to push for a decision.

  Zach does this over the phone pre-appointment only.
  Rick does it over the phone AND again in person before sitting down.
  Combined = maximum effectiveness.

## [IN-PERSON APPOINTMENT FLOW — Complete Process Map]
**Source:** 2026-05-28_In-Person Appointment Process Walkthrough.txt
**Insight:** Rick Ginn's complete appointment sequence (applies to virtual with
video call adaptations):

  STEP 1: CONDITIONING (over phone before appointment)
  STEP 2: ARRIVE EARLY — Drive around the block first.
    Observe: Are they cleaning up? How many cars? Neighborhood type?
    Rental community or owner-occupied? Busy street?
    Early arrival = free neighborhood comps data and seller psychology read.
    "On time" = 10-15 minutes early.
  STEP 3: MEET AND GREET at front door. Step back after ringing — Ring camera era.
    Dress rule: Clean casual. NOT a suit (looks like a slick salesman). NOT sloppy.
  STEP 4: WALK THE HOUSE — Say "Show me around."
    Look for rapport cues: family photos, sports teams, hobbies, pets.
    Use the SMELL of the house as data:
      Animal smell, mold smell, roach smell, wet/mildew = repair cost flags.
    If house is in bad shape: STAY IN IT. Let the discomfort surface the seller's
    emotional pain. This is what drives motivation to accept your offer.
    If house is in perfect shape: take them OUTSIDE immediately.
    "A perfect house increases the price if you keep talking about its features."
  STEP 5: TRANSITION — "Is there somewhere we can sit down?"
    They do not realize this begins negotiations. Mirror their posture (sit/stand).
  STEP 6: REAFFIRM SELLER'S OBJECTIVES (from qualifying call notes):
    "You told me you want to close by end of the month and move to your mother's
    in South Florida with no headaches. Is that still the goal?"
  STEP 7: PRESENT SOLUTION → VERBAL OFFER at LAO (never MAO).
  STEP 8: GO FOR NO → expect counter → TRIAL CLOSE loop.
    Example trial close: "If I can close in 30 days and cover closing costs,
    I can offer $155,000. Is that something we can work with?"
    Counter arrives → respond slowly (match their response time) → ask for
    something in return when you meet their counter.
    "That is stretching it for me. Tell you what — if you can sign right now, I can do it."
  STEP 9: WRITE CONTRACT only after verbal agreement.
    "The contract is not a negotiating tool. It is a summary of what you already agreed to."
  STEP 10: SET EXPECTATIONS:
    "The title company will reach out before end of day. I will check in tonight.
    Go ahead and start making moving plans."

  RULE: Never start at MAO. Always start at LAO. You can go up; you can never go down.
  If you accept too fast (even at a good price) → seller gets remorse → cancel.

## [QUALIFYING CASH BUYERS — 5 Questions + Proof of Legitimacy System]
**Source:** 2026-05-28_Qualifying Cash Buyers.txt
**Insight:** Zach's cash buyer qualifier (use before adding anyone to buyers list):

  QUESTION 1: "Are you looking to buy more houses for flipping or renting in the area?"
    If no → not active. Do not add.
  QUESTION 2: "Have you bought from a wholesaler before?"
    If no → yellow flag but do not dismiss. They need education before first deal.
  QUESTION 3: "Are you okay with us making an assignment fee?"
    If uncomfortable with fees over $10K → drop them immediately.
    They will be a problem at closing and will try to renegotiate your fee.
  QUESTION 4: "Give me your LLC name."
    Look up the LLC on the county property appraiser website.
    Real active buyers have purchase history in their LLC name on public deeds.
    This is more reliable than proof of funds (easily faked).

  QUESTION 5: "What price range, property type, and areas do you buy in?"
    Cross-reference with their actual purchase history.
    If they say "anywhere in Memphis" but all past purchases are suburban →
    they do not buy in inner-city zip codes. Do not send them those deals.

  CASH BUYER QUESTIONNAIRE FIELDS TO STORE:
    Flip or rent preference, target price range, preferred property types,
    desired areas, prior purchases (addresses + count), comfortable with
    assignment fees (yes/no), preferred communication (text/call/email),

## [DOUBLE CLOSING — Complete How-To and Funding Method]
**Source:** 2026-05-28_Double Closing.txt
**Insight:** Use double closing when assignment is not possible (state law,
non-assignable contract, seller uncomfortable with assignment, title company refuses).

  HOW IT WORKS (step by step):
    Step 1: Sign Purchase and Sales Agreement with seller (A→B = $100K)
    Step 2: Sign separate Purchase and Sales Agreement with cash buyer (B→C = $120K)
    Step 3: Cash buyer wires $120K into escrow 3-7 days before closing date
    Step 4: Title company uses buyer's $120K to fund your A-to-B contract ($100K)
    Step 5: Five minutes later, B-to-C closes — you receive $20K assignment fee

  FUNDING TRICK (avoid hard money):
    Get written consent from buyer to use their escrow funds to float your A-to-B.
    Most buyers agree because they get the deal they want and the money was
    sitting idle in escrow anyway. Title company orchestrates both closings same day.
    Requires title company cooperation — most investor-friendly title cos will do it.

  RULES:
    - Not advisable on deals under $10K profit (extra closing costs eat margin)
    - Total closing costs = approximately 1.5-2% of purchase price
    - Get reissue title credit passed to cash buyer = 50-70% discount on their title fees
    - You effectively pay only ONE set of closing fees total
    - You can own the property for literally one minute — legally identical to owning
      it for 100 years once title transfers (no "seasoning" requirement in most states)
    - Do NOT use for risky deals — double closing requires confirmed profit margin

  WHEN TO USE:
    - State restricts assignment of contracts
    - Realtor contract explicitly states "non-assignable"
    - Seller is uncomfortable seeing an assignment fee
    - Buyer has never done an assignment and is intimidated by it
    - You want to keep your purchase price private

  CONS:
    - Additional closing fees (offset by reissue title credit)
    - Some title companies will not do it
    - Not appropriate for thin deals (under $10K profit)
    - More complex to explain to first-time buyers

## [WHOLETAILING — When to Go Semi-Retail Instead of Pure Wholesale]
**Source:** 2026-05-28_Wholetailing.txt
**Insight:** Wholetailing = buy the property, do only cosmetic repairs, sell
slightly below retail within 90 days. Goal: 2x-2.5x your typical assignment fee.

  QUALIFICATION CRITERIA (less than 10% of deals):
    - High-demand property type in your specific market
      (Memphis example: 3/1 or 3/2 brick ranch in decent school district)
    - Only cosmetic repairs needed (under $10-20K maximum)
    - Strong local demand with low inventory
    - NO: bad curb appeal (cannot fix structural ugly with paint)
    - NO: major repairs (foundation, electrical, full roof replacement)

  90-DAY HARD RULE:
    If not sold by day 91 → wholesale it immediately. Never extend.
    Timeline math: 2 weeks repairs + 10 days to find buyer + 7-10 days
    inspection + 30-45 day close = approximately 90 days.
    Every day past 90 erodes profit. Holding costs compound faster than price gains.

  PROFIT TARGET: At minimum 2x, ideally 2.5x your assignment fee.
    Example: Would have been $20K assignment → target $40K-$50K wholetail profit.
    If you cannot project at least 2x, just assign the contract.

  PRICING STRATEGY: List just BELOW retail from day one.
    Not above with plan to reduce (you do not have time for that realtor game).
    One buyer, one shot, move fast.

  BUYER PREFERENCE:
    Cash buyer preferred. Or conventional with 20% down minimum.
    20% down means loan amount is 80% of sale price → property only needs to
    appraise at 80% of your sale price → almost always passes appraisal.
    Have buyer waive the appraisal contingency (the "out if it does not appraise" clause).
    NEVER accept FHA buyers (91-day seasoning requirement kills the deal timeline).

  COSMETIC REPAIR TOOLKIT:
    Pressure washer + dumpster + painter = the three essentials.
    Carpet (fast to install) over tile or laminate (slow).
    Appliances: in and out easily, high visual impact.
    Goal: "Sell the vision" — clean it enough to let buyers imagine the rest.
    Never take it all the way to full renovation (scope creep kills timeline).

  FUNDING: Requires short-term funding (hard money or soft money).
    Hard money: Focus on POINTS not interest rate. 4 points on $100K = $4K upfront.
    Negotiate prepayment penalty window to 30 days (standard is 6 months — avoid that).
    Soft money: Private lender (family/partner), secured with a mortgage and note,
    personally guaranteed. Better terms and less overhead than hard money.

**Apply to ODIN:** Add wholetailing evaluation flag to the deal analysis workflow.
When a deal shows spread > 2x typical assignment fee AND repairs are cosmetic only
AND property fits high-demand profile → ODIN flags it as a potential wholetail candidate.


## [ASSIGNMENT OF CONTRACT — 5 Key Clauses Most Wholesalers Miss]
**Source:** 2026-05-28_Signing the Assignment of Contract.txt
**Insight:** Rick Ginn's 14-clause assignment contract walkthrough. The five most
critical clauses that most wholesalers do not have:

  CLAUSE 7 — Expiration Buffer (the most important operational rule):
    Set assignee's closing date 7-10 days BEFORE your PSA expires.
    If buyer fails to fund → you still have time to find another buyer.
    "The assignment of contract supersedes your PSA's closing date."
    If you give them the same date, they can delay until the last second.

  CLAUSE 8 — Non-Refundable Deposit:
    $5,000 minimum deposit, wired (not check) within 24 hours of signing.
    Non-refundable if assignee fails to close.
    Minimum deposit = maximum commitment. Sub-$1K deposits = buyers who walk.
    "If someone won't leave $5K they are not serious."

  CLAUSE 9 — No Seller Contact:
    If assignee contacts the seller directly, the deal is void and they forfeit deposit.
    Prevents buyers from going around you and renegotiating directly with the seller.

  CLAUSE 12 — The Renegotiation Clause (Rick calls this his "$1M clause"):
    "If assigner renegotiates the purchase price downward, the difference goes
    to the assigner as an additional assignment fee, not to the assignee."
    Example: Locked at $100K, buyer paying $120K. You renegotiate seller to $95K.
    That extra $5K is YOURS, not the buyer's.
    Without this clause, buyers who find out about the price reduction claim the savings.
    Rick says this clause alone has earned him over $1,000,000.

  CLAUSE 13 — As-Is:
    Assigner makes no warranty on property condition. Assignee accepts as-is.
    Assignee does their own due diligence. Protects you from post-close lawsuits.

  ADDITIONAL RULES:
    Always use your own title company — never let the buyer bring theirs.

## [ARV COMPING — Exact Rules + Non-Disclosure States + XLeads MLS Access]
**Source:** 2026-05-28_How to Comp Properties.txt + 2026-05-28_How to Find ARV.txt
**Insight:** The complete comp methodology with specific rules and edge cases:

  ZACH'S COMPING RULES (applied in this order):
    1. Square footage: +/- 200 sq ft from subject property (not +/- 400 or 500)
    2. Location: 0.25 mile radius MAX, same subdivision when possible,
       never cross major streets, stay within same school district
    3. Bedrooms/bathrooms: Same bed/bath count
    4. Year built: +/- 10 years from subject property's construction date
    5. Sale date: Sold within the LAST 3 MONTHS (not 6, not 12 — 3)
    6. Property type: Single family only vs. subject

  ZILLOW AS COMPASS (not bible):
    Zestimate is directionally useful but off by 10-20% and usually HIGH.
    "Zillow will point you toward New York. The actual answer is Boston."
    Use it to validate your comp direction is not insane. Never use it as ARV.

  THE "CHEAT A LITTLE" RULE (legitimate shortcut):
    If a house is next door, same street, similar construction, sold within
    2-3 weeks — it is acceptable to use it as a comp even if bed/bath count
    is slightly different. Property itself previously selling (same house)
    is always the best comp when available. Regress/inflate for time.

  BEST COMP: THE PROPERTY ITSELF. If the subject property sold 3 years ago
  for $130K and values rose 60% since then → $130K x 1.6 = $208K ARV.

  NON-DISCLOSURE STATES (cannot see sold prices on Zillow/Redfin):
    Idaho, Kansas, Missouri, Mississippi, Louisiana, Wyoming, Utah, Texas,
    North Dakota, New Mexico, Montana.
    In these states, MLS access = $35-64/month per association PLUS you
    need a real estate license. Workaround: XLeads subscription includes
    full MLS access for one market with the basic plan.

  PROPERTY APPRAISER WEBSITE HACK (secret source of truth):
    County property appraiser is government data (99% accurate vs. Zillow's 80%).
    Reveals: exact sq footage (under-air only, NOT gross), year built, construction
    type, pool, roof type, permits history.
    SECRET TRICK: Check permit history to find actual roof age.
    If a house was built in 2002 and has no roof permit — the roof is 20+ years old.
    A Florida concrete tile roof over 20 years old drops property value $20-30K.
    Never ask the seller directly — they will lie. Pull the permit records instead.
    School district: A $20-30K price difference can exist between houses on
    the same street with different school districts (especially Texas).

  ARV CALCULATION FORMULA (after getting 4-5 comps):
    1. Take sold price per square foot for each comp
    2. Average them: e.g., $160 + $180 + $120 + $190 + $155 = $161/sqft average
    3. Multiply by subject property sq footage: $161 x 1,250 sqft = $201,250 ARV

  XLEADS COMPING WORKFLOW:
    → Open property → click Comparables → select Sold + Pending + Active
    → filter to 3 months, 0.25 mile, same bed/bath
    → XLeads auto-calculates average price per sqft and multiplies by subject
    → Download a comps report (sendable PDF for cash buyers and JV partners)

**Apply to ODIN:** When Brock enters a property address, ODIN prompt:
"Pull XLeads comps within 0.25 miles, same bed/bath, sold in last 90 days.
Also pull permit history from [county] property appraiser for roof age."
The permit-based roof age hack is a consistent negotiation lever to lower offer.


## [QUALIFYING SELLERS — MCTP Framework Deep Dive + Hidden Price Question Script]
**Source:** 2026-05-28_Qualifying Sellers.txt
**Insight:** Rick Ginn's complete MCTP framework with specific language for each pillar:

  ODN AVATAR: Off Market, Direct to seller, that Needs to sell.
  The opposite of MLS on-market. Never pursue "wants to sell" — only "needs to sell."

  THE 4 PILLARS IN ORDER:

  M — MOTIVATION (ask first, before condition):
    Question: "What is your reason for selling the house?"
    Then: ZIP your lip. Wait for the story.
    Goal: Get the full HUMAN STORY behind the house.
    "Real estate is just a symptom of human problems."
    Transactional wholesalers ask about price. Relational wholesalers ask for the story.
    The story is what allows you to solve their ACTUAL problem (not just price).

  C — CONDITION (open-ended, not a checklist):
    DO NOT ASK: "Is the roof good? Did you paint it?"
    ASK: "Tell me about the current state of the house. What's going on with it?"
    Then shut up. Let them talk for 80% of the time.
    If they say "it's great" → they do not trust you yet.
    If they go negative → they are unlocking true motivation.
    The more they talk about the bad condition, the more they want out.

  T — TIMEFRAME (most important pillar for identifying real deals):
    Question: "What is your ideal time frame to sell the property?"
    Rick's deal categories by timeframe:
      < 30 days: Drop everything. This is a hot deal.
      30-90 days: This is the norm. Full focus mode.
      90-180 days: Monthly check-in. Plant seeds.
      180+ days: Quarterly follow-up. Long-term nurture.
    SECRET QUESTION to unlock true timeframe:
      "Mr./Ms. Seller — if I was able to buy your house today,
      where would you and your family be relocating to?"
      ZIP YOUR LIP. Their first response is their truth.
      If they say "I haven't thought about it" → they are 180+ days out.
      If they say "My aunt's in Charlotte by end of the month" → < 30 days.

  P — PRICE (always last, after MCT):
    Never say "How much do you want?" or "What are you asking?"
    Those words trigger a retail pricing mindset.
    CORRECT PHRASING: "What do you need to get out from under this property?"
    "What do you need to get to move on away from this property?"
    If their price is outrageous:
      "How did you come up with that number?" → shut up and let them explain.
      Then: "I haven't found anything sold above $X in this area.
      That is what the house would be worth fully fixed up.
      Are you planning to fix it up and list it with a realtor?"
    This reframes their expectation without arguing.
    70% of deals come from FOLLOW-UP — if the price is too high now, they will
    come around later when circumstances change.

  80/20 RULE: You speak 20%, seller speaks 80%. Every call, every appointment.

**Apply to ODIN:** Add the "relocation question" to ODIN's call prompt script.
When Brock enters a new lead, ODIN should prompt: "Did you ask where they're
relocating to? That answer sets the follow-up priority tier."

## [CASH BUYER WALKTHROUGH PROTOCOL — Protecting the Deal]
**Source:** 2026-05-28_CASH BUYER WALKTHROUGHS + RULES.txt
**Insight:** Complete process for showing a property to a cash buyer:

  BEFORE THE WALKTHROUGH:
    1. Call buyer, pitch deal at MAO price
    2. If interested, email/text address and photos
    3. Follow up by NEXT DAY if no response — call back and ask "what number works?"
    4. Once buyer agrees to a price, confirm commitment:
       "Before I walk you through, I'm only showing this to 1-2 people.
       If you see it and it matches the pictures, can you commit to sign the
       assignment contract and wire the deposit that same day?"
    5. If buyer agrees → schedule the walk.
    6. If buyer hesitates → they are not committed. Do not waste the seller's time.

  TELLING THE SELLER:
    Do NOT ask permission. State it matter-of-factly:
    "Remember when we signed the agreement — I mentioned my partner and I
    would come by to take a look at the repairs. My partner John and I
    are going to stop by Tuesday afternoon. When's a good time?"
    Most sellers say yes. The framing as "partner" = normal business activity.

  DURING THE WALKTHROUGH:

    - Total negotiable fees: $600-$2,000 on a typical deal.

    If you wait: you lose your buyer, then your seller. Both disappear.

## [OFFER CALCULATION — LAO vs MAO + AI Calculator]

  FORMULA:
    MAO (Maximum Allowable Offer) = ARV × 0.70 - Repairs - Your Assignment Fee
    LAO (Least Allowable Offer) = 70-70% of MAO (this is your OPENING offer)

## [FILLING OUT THE PSA CONTRACT — Rick's Key Rules]
**Source:** 2026-05-28_Filling Out the Contract.txt
**Insight:** Rick Ginn's specific rules for writing the Purchase and Sale Agreement:

  DEPOSIT RULES:
    Minimum: $250-$500 (never $0 or $10 — looks illegitimate)
    Maximum risk deal: as low as $100 (signal you are not fully committed)
    Great deal: leave more (protects your position if seller gets cold feet)
    Standard MLS deposit: 1-2% of purchase price. Wholesalers use flat amounts.
    Frame to seller: "The deposit just opens escrow and gets title started.
    The real commitment is the $100K I'm handing you at closing."
    If seller demands over $1K deposit → red flag (usually means bad motivation).
    Never lose a deal over deposit — JV it or borrow the amount.

  CLOSING DATE:
    Minimum: 30 days (beginners need time to find buyer)
    Ideal: 60-90 days (more time = more buyer options)
    Reality: Most motivated sellers want 30 days or less
    Inspection period: NEVER match to closing date. Set at 50% of closing window.
    Example: 30-day contract → 15-day inspection period max.

  ASSIGNABILITY CLAUSE:
    Every wholesaling contract must say: "This contract is assignable by buyer."
    Only way it is NOT assignable is if the contract EXPLICITLY says "non-assignable."
    If it does not say non-assignable → it is legally assignable in most states.
    Zach uses: "This contract is assignable" to remove all doubt.
    Never negotiate on non-assignable contracts without a JV partner who can double close.

  LEGAL DESCRIPTION:
    Pull from county property appraiser website or the deed.
    Copy/paste exactly — do not rely on the mailing address alone.
    Option: Use the parcel/partial ID number (6-12 digit number) in place of full legal.

  TAX PRORATION:
    If you agree to pick up seller's back taxes, SPECIFY the maximum dollar amount.
    "Buyer agrees to pay 2024 property taxes not to exceed $3,000."
    Without a cap, you can inherit a $15K+ tax bill unexpectedly.

  EXIT STRATEGY (inspection period):
    Proper way to exit: Notify seller verbally + send text + send email + certified mail.
    Provide all evidence to title company that you properly exercised the inspection period.
    Reason to give seller: "There were unforeseen repair issues that changed the numbers."
    Never say you just cannot find a buyer or overpaid — frame it as discovery.

  SEND TO TITLE COMPANY IMMEDIATELY after signing. Do NOT wait for a buyer.

## [TAXES AND LLC SETUP — Rick's 3-Step Business Structure]
**Source:** 2026-05-28_How Taxes & LLC's Work for Wholesaling_.txt
**Insight:** Rick Ginn's LLC and tax strategy framework (23 years experience):

  THE 3 STEPS 98% OF WHOLESALERS SKIP:

    Banks will ask for it when opening business accounts.

    2. DIY QuickBooks bookkeeping without understanding how to build a balance sheet.
    3. Not making an IRS entity classification decision within the required window.

  KEY TAX INSIGHTS FOR WHOLESALERS:
    Section 199A deduction: 20% income deduction for qualified business income.
    Single filer: Available under $191,950/year.
    Joint filer: Available under $383,900/year.
    Car depreciation: Can depreciate your vehicle used for DFD, appointments, etc.
    Pre-tax dollar strategy: Vacation travel, training, equipment, corporate retreats
    can all be deductible with proper classification.
    401K as business owner: Can set up and contribute pre-tax + company match.
    HSA health savings account: Pre-tax medical expense savings.

    Creates LLC, sets up classification correctly, bookkeeping, files both personal
    and business returns as a unified strategy. Designed specifically for wholesalers.

  IRS PROGRESSIVE BRACKETS (2025):
    Under 22%: Most wholesalers paying taxes correctly

    37%: Top bracket — hits at ~$580K+ household income
