Step 1 — Finding the Right Business to Build

Not sure what business to start, or want to validate an idea before investing time in it? This is where ODIN starts.

Business Scout
scout [keywords or idea]
Tell ODIN what you're thinking about — or just a topic area — and it evaluates options and ranks them for you. It scores each idea on: how much money to start, how many hours per week it realistically takes, income potential at 3 and 6 months, competition level, and how well it fits your actual situation (your skills, interests, budget, and available time).
What you get back: Top 3 ranked business ideas with a score, startup cost estimate, monthly income potential, and honest reasoning for each. No fluff — real numbers and real tradeoffs.
Example Conversations
→
scout etsy printables digital products — Katelyn types this
→
ODIN: "Option 1: Digital Planners (Score 8.4/10) — $0 to start, $300–900/mo by month 3, low competition in niche categories like ADHD planners or teacher planners..." (continues with full analysis)
→
scout online business hours:15 budget:300
→
ODIN: Scopes ideas specifically to 15 hrs/week and $300 budget — narrows options to realistic matches

Step 2 — Modeling the Money

Before you commit to anything, see exactly what the income looks like — month by month, at your actual hours and budget.

Income Calculator
income [business name] hours:15 budget:300
Models realistic revenue at your hours and budget. Shows you month 1, month 3, month 6, and month 12 projections — not best-case fantasy numbers, but realistic ramp with honest assumptions stated. Also tells you what the main income levers are (what you'd need to do to double it) and where the ceiling is for that model.
What you get back: Month-by-month income projections, breakeven point, the 2-3 things that move the needle fastest, and a realistic ceiling for the business model.
Example
→
income digital planners on etsy hours:12 budget:200
→
Month 1: ~$80 (learning, first listings). Month 3: ~$400 (SEO starting to work, 8–12 listings). Month 6: ~$900 (repeat buyers, seasonal picks up). Month 12: $1,400–2,200 (if you build a catalog of 30+ products).
→
Main levers: Listing count, SEO keyword targeting, seasonal product timing.

Step 3 — Building the Launch Plan

Once you've picked a business and seen the income model, ODIN builds your exact week-by-week action plan.

Business Builder
build [business name]
Creates a detailed week-by-week task breakdown to launch the business from scratch. Every task has a priority, an estimated time, and a sequence so you always know what to do next. Built specifically around your available hours and budget — it won't tell you to spend $1,000 on tools if your budget is $200.
What you get back: Weeks 1–8 task list, prioritized and sequenced. What to do, in what order, roughly how long each step takes.
Example Output for "Digital Planners"
→
Week 1: Research 20 top-selling planner types on Etsy. Pick your niche. Free Canva account. Design 3 test products. (~5 hrs)
→
Week 2: Open Etsy shop. Write keyword-rich listings for all 3. Price at $3–5. First 10 mockup images. (~4 hrs)
→
Week 3–4: Create 5 more products. Start Pinterest board. Ask 3 friends to buy and review. (~6 hrs/week)
→
Week 5–8: Scale to 20 products. Target seasonal keywords. Track what's selling and double down.

Step 4 — Automation Audit

Once the business is live, ODIN maps every task and identifies what it can take off your plate.

Automation Auditor
audit [business name]
Goes through every recurring task in your business and categorizes it: what stays manual (requires your creativity/judgment), what ODIN can automate (email responses, content posting, reporting, customer follow-ups), and what tools handle the rest. Gives you a map of exactly what your automated version of the business looks like vs what you do today.
What you get back: Full task map (manual vs automated), estimated hours saved per week, and a list of exactly what to build in ODIN to make it happen.
